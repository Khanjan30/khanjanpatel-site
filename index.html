How I think

Same habit, different courts.

For 12 years I played tennis at the national level. You don't win at that level by hitting harder. You win by studying the other player's habits and adjusting before the score shows it.

I've used that habit everywhere since. At C1, it's how I noticed that a small group of customers was holding up most of the revenue. In the desert at Khavda, it meant learning how clean power is actually made before it ever reaches the grid.

Now I'm pointing it at the grid. Its hardest problem is timing, a few hours each day when everyone needs power at once.

Step 3

See clean energy built at scale

On the ground in western India · Feb to May 2026

Before Cornell Tech, I spent four months at Khavda, the world's largest renewable energy park. It's rising out of the salt desert of Kutch in western India, planned at 30 gigawatts of solar and wind across an area about five times the size of Paris.

I wanted to learn renewable power where it's actually built, not from reports. On site I saw what it takes to turn open desert into a working power system, from the panels and batteries to the lines that carry the power out.
